## Summary

- **PBD clustered shape matching with a rigid goal.** Overlapping clusters are projected in parallel (Jacobi). Corrections are averaged as $\Delta\mathbf x_i/n_i$ and scaled by a per-cluster stiffness $K$. Velocities are re-derived as $(\mathbf x^*-\mathbf x)/\Delta t$. The goal is $\mathbf g_i = R\,F_P\,\mathbf q_i + \mathbf c$, a *rigid* rotation of the plastic rest shape. There is no Müller-$\beta$ stretch blend.
- **Plastic state is a per-cluster matrix $F_P$.** It is folded into the fit as $A_{pq}=\sum m(\mathbf p-\mathbf c)\mathbf q^T F_P^T$ and $A_{qq}=F_P(\sum m\mathbf q\mathbf q^T)F_P^T$. The particles' rest positions are never moved, except when clusters are re-created.
- **Choi's optimal stretch.** Given $R$, the stretch is the least-squares *symmetric* $S$, solved in the eigenbasis of $A_{qq}$: $S = V_{qq}[(V_{qq}^T S_{pq} V_{qq})\circ\bar\Lambda]V_{qq}^T$, $\bar\Lambda_{ij}=2/(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)$. This handles anisotropic clusters cleanly.
- **Yield and flow rule.** The strain is the log principal strain $\|\epsilon\|=\max_i|\log\lambda_i|$. When it exceeds $\epsilon_Y$, a fraction $\gamma=\min(\nu(\|\epsilon\|-\epsilon_Y)/\|\epsilon\|,1)$ moves into $F_P$ through a volume-preserving multiplicative update $F_P\leftarrow V(\det\Lambda^{-1/3}\Lambda)^\gamma V^TF_P$.
  - Work hardening: $\epsilon_Y \mathrel{+}= \kappa\|\epsilon\|\Delta t$.
  - Stability clamp: each eigen-increment stays in $[0.8,1.2]$, so $F_P$ changes by at most about 20% per step.
- **Main claim: clamping $F_P$ is a dead end.** Without re-sampling, an unclamped $F_P$ becomes unstable and a clamped $F_P$ cannot deform further. The paper's answer is to re-sample instead of clamping.
- **Particle re-sampling.**
  - Delete a particle if it is closer than $0.1r$ to a lower-index neighbour, or if it is outside the mesh inflated by $0.25r$ and no vertex references it.
  - Seed up to $T=5$ Poisson candidates in the $[r,2r]$ shell around each particle of any cluster with $\|F_P\|_F>1.8$.
- **Cluster re-sampling.**
  - Delete a cluster if its member count leaves $[0.5\times,2\times]$ of the initial count, if $\|F_P\|_F>2.0$, or if all of its members are over-covered.
  - Re-create clusters with Poisson-spaced centres ($r^G=3r$) and radius membership ($r^C=4r$). New clusters start with $F_P=I$. The old plastic stretch is *embedded* into $\mathbf q=W_{new}(\mathbf x-\mathbf c)$.
  - $W_{new}$ comes from overlap-weighted averaging of $G=\tfrac12(U^TU-I)$, with $U=A_{pq}A_{qq}^{-1}F_P$ and eigenvalues clamped to $[0.25,4]$.
- **Skinning to particles, not clusters.** $\mathbf y=\sum_a w_a(Q_{l_a}\mathbf z_a+\mathbf x_{l_a})$, where the particle orientation $Q_i$ is the normalized average of its clusters' quaternions. Explicit surface tracking handles topology. An optional per-component volume fix moves vertices along their normals.
- **Cost.** The GPU simulation takes 1.8–4.8 ms for 4.5k–38k particles. Run time is dominated by surface tracking and ray-cast inside/outside tests, which this repo does not need.

## Useful content

**1. Plastic state inside the fit (Eqs. 1–2).** $F_P$ multiplies $\mathbf q$ before both moment matrices, so $A=A_{pq}A_{qq}^{-1}$ maps the *plastic* rest to the current pose and $R,S$ measure only the elastic part. This is algebraically what the repo already does: `applyPlasticity` sets `qx = Sp·q0` (`shape-match-core.js:384-389`), and `matchCluster` builds `Apq` from `qx` (`:328-333`). The repo also mutates `q0` (rotation bake), which has no counterpart in the paper.

**2. Optimal stretch (Eqs. 3–5) and degenerate clusters.** Solve $SA_{qq}+A_{qq}S = R^TA_{pq}+A_{pq}^TR$ in the $A_{qq}$ eigenbasis. When one eigenvalue $\lambda^{qq}_k\approx 0$ (a *planar* cluster), the $k$-th row and column of $(V^TS_{pq}V)$ are also ≈0. The natural regularization is to set $\tilde S_{kk}=1$ and $\tilde S_{ik}=0$, so the cluster gives "no information, no stretch" along its normal. This is the piece this repo is missing most (see the planar-cluster probe below).

**3. Strain, yield and flow.**
- $\epsilon_l = V\log\Lambda V^T$ and $\|\epsilon_l\|=\max_i|\log\lambda_i|$. This is the true principal strain, so a crush along one axis is not diluted by the other two axes the way a Frobenius norm is.
- $\gamma=\min\big(\nu(\|\epsilon\|-\epsilon_Y)/\|\epsilon\|,1\big)$ moves only the *excess over yield*. With $\nu\to1$ this is a one-step return to the yield surface, so the elastic springback after unloading is bounded by $\epsilon_Y$. That matches how sheet metal behaves.
- Paper values: $\epsilon_Y\in\{10^{-4},10^{-2},0.1\}$ and $\nu\in\{0.1,0.2,0.5,0.9,0.99\}$. $\epsilon_Y=10^{-4}$ is "immediately plastic" (taffy). $\epsilon_Y=0.1$ is "10% elastic range" (dough).

**4. Volume.** The paper's increment is exactly isochoric, $\det\big((\det\Lambda)^{-1/3}\Lambda\big)=1$. A car cluster stands for a *hollow* box, so crushing it must lose enclosed volume. Keep the repo's partial approach (`keep = 0.08`, `shape-match-core.js:360-366`), but consider splitting volumetric and deviatoric yield, as Candidate 9 below does.

**5. Work hardening.** $\epsilon_Y\leftarrow\epsilon_Y+\kappa\|\epsilon_l\|\Delta t$ with $\kappa\in\{0,0.1,1,5\}$ (Fig. 3). For a crumple zone this gives the rising force–deflection curve once folds stack up.

**6. Per-step plastic clamp.** Each eigenvalue of the increment is clamped to $[0.8,1.2]$. This is a cheap, explicit bound on how much permanent shape can change in one frame of a violent hit.

**7. Re-embedding plastic state (§3.3.3/§3.3.5).**
- $W=F_P^{-1}A_{qq}A_{pq}^{-1}$ maps world space to the cluster's pre-plastic local space.
- For a fresh cluster: $U=W^{-1}=A_{pq}A_{qq}^{-1}F_P$ and $G=\frac12(U^TU-I)$. Average $G$ with overlap weights and form $2G_{new}+I=VDV^T$.
- Clamp each $\lambda$ to $[0.25,4]$. Then $W_{new}=V\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda^{-1/2})V^T$, $\mathbf q=W_{new}(\mathbf x-\mathbf c)$, and recentre.
- Overlap weight for two radius-$r^C$ spheres at distance $d<2r^C$ (standard geometry, not given in the paper): $V=\pi(4r^C+d)(2r^C-d)^2/12$.
- The key idea for this repo: when plastic deformation gets large, **reset $F_P$ to $I$ and move the deformation into the rest offsets** instead of clamping $F_P$.

**8. Cluster validity rules.**
- Delete if the member count leaves $[0.5\times, 2\times]$ of the initial count, if $\|F_P\|_F>2.0$, or if the cluster is redundant (all members are in more than $M_{maxother}$ clusters).
- Note that $\|I\|_F=\sqrt3\approx1.732$, so the thresholds 1.8 and 2.0 are only about 4% and 15% above identity. Compression *lowers* $\|F_P\|_F$ unless volume is preserved. For this repo's non-isochoric $S_p$, use singular values of $S_p$ instead.

**9. Cluster construction.** Pick centres with Poisson spacing ($r^G=3r$) and take every particle within $r^C=4r$ as a member. This is symmetric by construction and gives volumetric clusters (56–127 particles per cluster in Table 1), never planar ones. With this repo's particle radii of 0.26–0.5 m (`MASS_SPECS`, `streamed-deform.ts:156-176`), $r^C=4r$ comes to 1.0–2.0 m. That is the same scale as the existing 1.45 m skin radius (`streamed-deform.ts:593`).

**10. Skinning to particles (§3.2).** Per-particle orientation is the normalized quaternion average over the particle's clusters [Gra01]. Each vertex then binds to its 4 nearest particles with inverse-distance weights and rotated local offsets. Positional detail that lives in the particles, such as a single bumper particle pushed in, then reaches the mesh even when no cluster fit can express it.

### Probe evidence (throwaway, not committed)

- **P1 — planar clusters cannot rotate or yield.** I computed the $A_{qq}$ eigenvalues for all 22 clusters of `new StreamedDeformation(BoxGeometry)`. Ten clusters are exactly planar ($\lambda_{min}=0$): `[bumperFL,bumperFR,engineL,engineR]`, `[bumperRL,bumperRR,axleR]`, `[engineL,railL,wingFL]`×3, `[engineR,railR,wingFR]`×3, `[axleR,tank,bumperRL/RR]`. Near-planar ones: `[bumperFL,wingFL,engineL,railL]` at 1.7e-3, `[railL,wingFL,doorL,cell]` at 3.7e-3, `[bumperRL,doorL,tank,axleR]` at 1.5e-6.
  - In a 24-frame shape-mode frontal crash (14 m/s, centred and offset 0.5), all 10 planar clusters had `|det A|<1e-9` in **24/24** frames, with R angle 0, `‖S−I‖=0` and `‖Sp−I‖=0`. They act as translation-only springs back to the pristine shape and never yield. This includes the front bumper box.
  - Unit check: a 3-particle planar cluster yawed 0.5 rad gives `R` angle 0.000 and a goal error of 0.318 m.
  - Prototype fix: add $\hat{\mathbf n}_{cur}\hat{\mathbf n}_{rest}^T$ to `A` before `m3Polar`, where $\hat{\mathbf n}_{cur}=\mathrm{normalize}(A\mathbf u\times A\mathbf v)$. This recovers 0.500 rad with zero goal error. Under a 0.7 squash it reports `‖S−I‖=0.299`, so the cluster can now yield.
- **P2 — plastic cap saturation.** The same crash with `squash=0.4, buckle=0.45` gives `maxE = 0.58`.
  - Centred hit: all 12 non-planar clusters end at `‖Sp−I‖_F = 0.52–0.58`, i.e. at the cap. Offset 0.5 hit: 8 of 12 are at 0.50–0.58; the others are at 0.14–0.38.
  - Centred hit, max R angles: 9 of 12 clusters reach 0.77–0.87 rad, pinned near the 0.85 clamp; the other three reach 0.16–0.53. Offset hit max: 0.90.
  - Max `‖S−I‖` reaches 1.77, just under the 1.8 hard reset in `m3Polar`.
- **P3 — rotation bake.** With `Sp=I`, rotating `q0` by 0.6× the held yaw changes goals by 1.4e-16 at 0.5 rad and by 0.265 m at 1.2 rad (where the clamp engages). A held 0.6 rad yaw plus 0.6 squash over 60 contacting steps shrinks `Σ|q0|` to 0.9405 of its start value, because of the lerp.
- **P4 — mirror asymmetry.** I compared ±0.55 m offset frontals over 30 frames and summed the mirror mismatch of bumper/rail/door/hub/wing displacements:
  - as-is: **0.724 m**;
  - `clusterBeta` using each cluster's own cage absorption: 0.608;
  - `glassFront` cluster made symmetric (`+railR`): 0.023;
  - both fixes: **0.001**.
- **P5 — Sp update order.** I iterated toward a held shear+squash target. `Sp·ΔS` and `ΔS·Sp` converge identically (`‖S−I‖` 3.5e-4 → 7e-16 over 200 steps).
- **P6 — skin drops Sp.** A cluster with `Sp=diag(1,1,0.6)` and particles held at the plastically squashed shape: `matchSkinLocal` gives `skinM = I`, and a rest vertex at z = 0.9 renders at **0.8997** instead of the plastic **0.560**.
- **Bench baseline** (`npm run bench`): `m3Polar` 382 ns, `matchCluster` 581 ns, `applyPlasticity` 348 ns, `transformSkinPointInto` 7.8 ns per op.

## Not applicable / caveats

- **Particle re-sampling (§3.3.1–3.3.2) does not fit.**
  - The 20 particles are *named roles*: `bumperFL`, `hubFL`, `cell`, … in `MASS_SPECS` (`streamed-deform.ts:156`). Hull extraction, `followGroup`, sensors, beams and hub/suspension logic all look them up by name. Seeding or deleting particles would break all of that.
  - Sheet metal does not flow apart the way dough does, so under-sampling is not the failure mode here. The failures here are degenerate cluster geometry and the plastic cap.
  - Ray-cast inside tests were about 60% of the paper's re-sampling time, which is unaffordable at 24 cars with a 15 ms deform budget.
- **Explicit surface tracking and topology change ([CMMK15], §3.3.7) do not fit.** The body is a procedural loft (`car-mesh.ts:84 PROFILE`, `:368 makeChassisGeometry`), and tearing is authored as detachable parts (`car.ts:952 detachPart`). Remeshing would break that pipeline, and it cost 7–35 ms per object in the paper's Table 1.
- **Visual-mesh volume conservation [TWGT10] and the fully isochoric plastic increment are wrong for a hollow car body.** A crushed nose must lose enclosed volume. Keep partial volume preservation (`keep = 0.08`) or the split yield from item 9.
- **Frobenius-norm thresholds on $F_P$ (1.8 / 2.0) assume det ≈ 1.** This repo's `Sp` loses volume, which lowers $\|F_P\|_F$ under crush. Use singular values or the conditioning of `A_qq` instead.
- **The paper's yield values are for soft materials.** It used $\epsilon_Y$ = 1e-4 for taffy and 0.1 for dough. Car metal at game scale needs a yield that still lets light taps spring back.
- **The PBD velocity update $\mathbf v=(\mathbf x^*-\mathbf x)/\Delta t$ should not be adopted blindly.** The repo deliberately leaves velocities alone after the shape correction (`writeShapeToMasses` 725). That makes the shape constraint dissipative, with no rebound after a crash. Adopting the paper's update would add elastic bounce unless damping is added too.
- **Rigid goal (β = 0).** Today's contacting β of 0.03–0.27 (`clusterBeta` 1981) partly hides the fact that 10 clusters never yield. Only drop β toward the paper's rigid goal after items 1 and 8.
- **Uncertain reconstructions.**
  - Eq. 4 is reconstructed. The text extraction lost it, so I used $S_{pq}=\mathrm{sym}(R^TA_{pq})$, which is consistent with Eq. 5.
  - The $10^{-2}$ / $10^{-4}$ exponents in Figs. 2, 3, 5 and 6 are reconstructed.
  - The paper does not say what happens when γ ≤ 0; I assumed no flow.
- **Not covered by the paper:** cluster fracture (it defers to [Cho14] / [JML 16]) and anything about rotation clamps. The repo's 0.85 rad clamp and its rotation bake are Bugbear/Rajala-specific and have no counterpart here.
- **The GPU timings (TitanX, thousands of particles) are not comparable** to this repo's CPU JS kernel with 20 particles per car.
