# Strain Based Dynamics (Müller et al., SCA 2014) — analysis for crash-deformer-test

## Summary

- Replaces PBD edge-distance constraints with **one constraint per entry of the Green–St Venant strain tensor** of an element (tet or triangle): $\mathbf F=\mathbf P\mathbf Q^{-1}$, $\mathbf S=\mathbf F^T\mathbf F$, three stretch constraints on $S_{ii}$ and three shear constraints on $S_{ij}$.
- **Each strain component has its own stiffness $k_{ij}$** → anisotropy (stiff along one axis, soft along another, independently soft shear) on any tessellation.
- Rotation invariance comes from Green strain itself — **no polar decomposition** per element.
- **Stretch in square-root form** $\sqrt{S_{ii}}-s_i$ (= length of the deformed material axis minus rest stretch) is linear along its gradient → solved in one projection, ~25% less residual strain at equal iterations, no overshoot.
- **Normalized shear** $\tilde S_{ij}=\hat{\mathbf f}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf f}_j$ (cosine between deformed material axes) decouples shear resistance from stretch.
- **Rest stretches $s_i$** are explicit targets; changing them is how strain limits / non-unit rest states are expressed (the natural hook for plasticity).
- **General strain orientation**: rotate each element's rest coordinates into a chosen local frame so "x/y/z" stiffness means "along the rail / across the panel / along the normal".
- **Volume/area constraint** $\det\mathbf F - s_1s_2s_3$ with its own stiffness; signed, so it **un-inverts** elements. Over-constraint jitter when strain and volume stiffness are both ≈1 → soften volume slightly.
- **Mode damping**: $\mathbf v_i \mathrel{-}= k(\sum_j\mathbf v_j\cdot\mathbf n_j)\mathbf n_i$ along the constraint's correction direction only; never damps rigid motion.
- Cost: ~30% slower than edge-based PBD per element, >3× faster than continuum strain limiting [TPS09].

## Useful content

For a car body built from a handful of control particles, clusters and FFD cages:

1. **Per-axis strain stiffness in a material frame** (Eqs. 9–10, 14–15, §3.6). A crash rail should be soft in axial compression (crumple), stiff in cross-section and bending; a door skin soft along its normal (intrusion), stiff in-plane; the roof stiff in-plane, soft vertically under rollover. That is one material with different $k_{xx},k_{yy},k_{zz},k_{xy},k_{xz},k_{yz}$ in the part's rest frame. Shape matching already computes the needed quantity: the polar stretch $\mathbf S$ of $\mathbf A=\mathbf R\mathbf S$ satisfies $\mathbf S^2=\mathbf A^T\mathbf A$, i.e. it **is** $\sqrt{\mathbf F^T\mathbf F}$ in the cluster's rest frame — the paper's "square root of strain" (§3.4). Diagonal entries of $\mathbf S-\mathbf I$ are the paper's $\sqrt{S_{ii}}-1$ stretches (up to rotation of principal axes); off-diagonals measure shear.
2. **Plasticity = moving the rest stretch targets $s_i$** (Eqs. 9, 14, 18). Per-component yield: when $|\sqrt{S_{ii}}-s_i|$ exceeds a per-axis yield, move $s_i$ toward the current value (creep), clamped by a per-axis limit — compression-only for axial crush, small tension cap. Shear yield via the normalized shear $\tilde S_{ij}$, limited by an angle ($\cos$ of max shear angle).
3. **Volume as a separate, softenable constraint** (Eqs. 16–22). For metal crush, volume is *not* conserved in crumple zones (folds collapse the box) but should be roughly conserved in the safety cell; per-part volume stiffness is the right knob. Signed-volume target also gives a principled anti-inversion floor.
4. **Normalized shear without diagonal edges** (Eq. 15). A rectangular crumple box made of four edge springs has zero shear resistance (parallelogram mode); an angle constraint resists shear without adding axial stiffness — the same thing a diagonal truss would do but without coupling into crush.
5. **Stability tricks**: square-root stretch avoids overshoot (Eq. 14 vs 13); lower shear stiffness because the normalized shear is non-linear; soften volume to avoid jitter; damping only along constraint modes (Eq. 23).
6. **Positions and velocities must be consistent**: PBD sets $\mathbf v=(\mathbf p-\mathbf x)/\Delta t$ after projection. Any position-only correction that leaves velocity untouched fights the next integration step.

## Not applicable / caveats

- **Element meshes.** SBD is formulated over tetrahedra/triangles with exact $\mathbf F=\mathbf P\mathbf Q^{-1}$. This car has 20 control particles and overlapping least-squares clusters; there are no tets. Building a tet mesh would replace, not refine, the Müller 2005 core. The transferable ideas are per-component stiffness, rest-stretch plasticity, volume stiffness and mode damping, applied to cluster `S`.
- **"No polar decomposition" is not a win here.** Shape matching needs `R` anyway (goal = `R·…`, rotation-in-plasticity, normal transform), so the paper's main perf argument does not apply. With 20 particles per car, solver cost is negligible next to skinning and `computeVertexNormals` (~15 ms in fleet crashes).
- **Volume conservation is mostly wrong for crushed sheet metal.** Hollow crumple boxes lose enclosed volume when they fold. Use the volume constraint only as a weak, per-part term (cell/roof) and as an anti-inversion floor, never at stiffness ≈1 (the paper warns of jitter there too).
- **Bending (§3.8)** needs a simulated surface mesh; the visible panel is skinned from cages and folds are procedural (`skin` accordion). Dihedral constraints have no particles to act on. Panel-level folding would need a separate low-res shell, which this prototype does not have.
- **Skin sliding / cloth use cases** (Figs. 1, 3, 5) have no counterpart.
- **Stiffness meaning.** As the paper notes, $k$ depends on time step and iteration count. The engine varies iterations (`stiffnessIters`) and substeps (`physicsSlice`), so per-axis weights tuned at one setting will drift. Tune them with the iteration count fixed (contact path already uses `iters = 2`, `streamed-deform.ts:2008`).
- **Normalized shear overshoot.** Eq. (15) is non-linear along its gradient. With the shape match's 0.14 m per-iteration step clamp (`streamed-deform.ts:2056-2057`) this is bounded, but in lattice `stepBeams` it is not — keep shear k low there.
- Equations in the Appendix of the rewrite (shear gradient, triangle frame, bending modes) are partly reconstructed; re-derive before implementing candidate 5 (Eq. 36 was derived independently from Eq. 15 and checked only symbolically).
