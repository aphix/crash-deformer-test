# Position Based Dynamics (Müller et al. 2006) — analysis for crash-deformer-test

## Summary

- **Predict → project → derive velocity.** Each step: apply external forces to $\mathbf{v}$, damp, predict $\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{x}+\Delta t\,\mathbf{v}$, project constraints on $\mathbf{p}$ for $n_s$ iterations, then set $\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{x})/\Delta t$, $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{p}$, then fix velocities of colliding vertices (friction/restitution). Position corrections therefore *are* the dynamics; they feed back into velocity.
- **General constraint projection.** Any scalar $C(\mathbf{p}_1..\mathbf{p}_n)$ (equality $=0$ or inequality $\ge 0$) is solved with one Newton step along the gradient, mass-weighted: $\Delta\mathbf{p}_i=-s\,w_i\nabla_{\mathbf{p}_i}C$, $s=C/\sum_j w_j|\nabla_{\mathbf{p}_j}C|^2$. For internal constraints this conserves linear and angular momentum (no "ghost forces").
- **Non-linear Gauss–Seidel.** Constraints are projected one after another and see each other's results immediately (faster propagation than Jacobi); order matters, the last constraint is strongest, and a varying order can oscillate when over-constrained.
- **Iteration-independent stiffness.** Scaling each correction by $k$ gives residual $(1-k)^{n_s}$; using $k'=1-(1-k)^{1/n_s}$ makes the residual $(1-k)$ regardless of $n_s$. Stiffness still depends on $\Delta t$ (fine with fixed steps).
- **Collisions as inequality constraints** generated once per step outside the solver: continuous (ray $\mathbf{x}\to\mathbf{p}$ entry point $\mathbf{q}_c$, normal $\mathbf{n}_c$, $C=(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}_c)\cdot\mathbf{n}_c$, $k=1$) with static closest-point fallback; friction/restitution applied in the post-update velocity pass. Two-way coupling to rigid bodies by applying impulse $m_i\Delta\mathbf{p}_i/\Delta t$ to the partner.
- **Rigid-mode-preserving damping.** Compute $\mathbf{v}_{cm}$, $\boldsymbol\omega=\mathbf{I}^{-1}\mathbf{L}$; damp only $\Delta\mathbf{v}_i=\mathbf{v}_{cm}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{v}_i$ by $k_{damping}$. $k=1$ turns the set into a rigid body without losing spin.
- **Attachment** = set position to target (static/kinematic) and $w_i=0$ so no other constraint moves it.
- **Unconditional stability** comes from never extrapolating: instability can only come from the solver/constraint shape, not $\Delta t$. One iteration ≈ explicit, many iterations ≈ implicit/stiff.
- Cloth specifics: edge distance constraints (Jakobsen form, Eq. 10/11), stretch-independent dihedral bending, triangle-side constraints with thickness for self collision, global volume constraint for balloons, tearing by vertex split when stretch exceeds a threshold. Slides add a tetrahedral volume constraint.

## Useful content

For a real-time car body driven by 20 control particles:

1. **Distance-constraint projection (Eqs. 10–11)** — the mass-weighted split $\Delta\mathbf{p}_1=-\frac{w_1}{w_1+w_2}(|\mathbf{p}_{12}|-d)\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, $\Delta\mathbf{p}_2=+\frac{w_2}{w_1+w_2}(\ldots)\hat{\mathbf{n}}$. As an *inequality* with a plastic rest length $d=l_{plastic}$ ($C=|\mathbf{p}_{12}|-l_{plastic}\ge 0$ for crush stops, $C=l_{max}-|\mathbf{p}_{12}|\ge0$ for stretch limits) it is exactly what a crumple beam with yield needs, with unconditional stability at any stiffness.
2. **$k'=1-(1-k)^{1/n_s}$** — lets the shape-match `squash` slider mean the same stiffness no matter how many iterations `stiffnessIters` picks. Generalised to variable $\Delta t$ (our engine has variable slices), the per-call factor $k_{call}=1-(1-k_{ref})^{\Delta t/(\Delta t_{ref} n_s)}$ removes both iteration- and step-rate dependence. (The time extension is my derivation from the paper's residual argument, not in the paper.)
3. **Momentum conditions (Eqs. 1–2)**: $\sum m_i\Delta\mathbf{p}_i=0$ and $\sum\mathbf{r}_i\times m_i\Delta\mathbf{p}_i=0$ — a cheap invariant to measure/enforce after any non-physical position edit (goal averaging, half-space clamps, step clamps).
4. **Velocity update $\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{x})/\Delta t$** — makes shape-match corrections transfer momentum between particles (spring-back, rear masses decelerated by the body instead of only by contact). Only safe when $\Delta\mathbf{p}$ is produced *within* the step from the predicted position (so $\Delta\mathbf{p}\propto\Delta t$); per-frame-sized corrections divided by a tiny slice $\Delta t$ explode (the repo already hit this — see `feedOverlap` comment).
5. **Damping §3.5** — kill internal jiggle after a crash while preserving the wreck's linear *and angular* velocity (spin-outs). Cost: one 3×3 inverse per car per slice, trivial for 20 particles.
6. **Collision response split**: position projection with $k=1$ + velocity pass with restitution/friction only on vertices that had a collision constraint this step. Our `sphereHit` and ground clamps follow this pattern already.
7. **Gauss–Seidel vs Jacobi**: sequential cluster projection propagates a crush front across the body in one sweep; Jacobi averaging (what we do) needs more iterations for the same reach. Keep a fixed order (or a fixed symmetric forward/backward pattern) to avoid L/R bias and oscillation.
8. **Tearing rule** (stretch > threshold → split): matches our beam break (`len > rest*2.2`) and is a natural rule for hinge/part detachment by constraint strain rather than ad-hoc impulse thresholds.
9. **Attachment by $w_i=0$**: the planted-hub logic is already an attachment in PBD terms (hubs keep full mass in the cluster fit but receive no correction). Keep that formulation if the shape step is rewritten (e.g. Gauss–Seidel), rather than dropping hubs from the clusters.

## Not applicable / caveats

- **Cloth-specific constraints** (dihedral bending, triangle-side self collision with thickness, balloon volume, vertex-split tearing) assume a simulated triangle mesh. Our rendered mesh is skinned from 16 cages and the per-cage + extra clusters over 20 particles (`skin`, `:2450`), so there are no simulated panel vertices to bend or tear. Bending only becomes relevant if a per-panel particle layer is added.
- **Continuous collision via rays $\mathbf{x}\to\mathbf{p}$** does not map onto the current pipeline. Car–car contact is 2D SAT on hulls (`pair-contact.ts resolveCarPair`, `sat.ts`), with sphere–sphere mass overlap as a secondary layer, and tunnelling is prevented by `physicsSlice` limiting travel to 0.07 m per slice (`sat.ts:23`).
- **"Unconditionally stable"** is about the integrator. Our blow-up risks are in the polar decomposition and the large-`A` cases, which are already guarded in `m3Polar` (`shape-match-core.js:102`) and by the `m3MaxAbs` checks in `matchCluster` (`:334`, `:339`). PBD does not remove the need for those guards.
- **Plain-PBD stiffness is not physical.** It depends on iteration count (fixable with $k'$) and on Δt. For physically meaningful beam or cluster stiffness use XPBD compliance (sibling paper) rather than $k$.
- **Two-way rigid coupling (§4.2)** assumes a separate rigid solver. Our car rigid state is *extracted from* the masses (`followGroup`, `:1123`), so the masses are the rigid body and no partner impulse is needed for intra-car constraints. Only `separateAlong` (`:1248`) is an external position edit that skips the partner-impulse step, and pair impulses are handled elsewhere.
- **A global volume constraint (Eq. 14)** fits a closed shell under pressure. A car must lose volume when it crushes. Our partial `det` restoration in `applyPlasticity` (`keep = 0.08`) is the right relaxation, and a hard $k=1$ volume constraint would fight the crumple.
- **Gauss–Seidel order dependence**: with 20 particles and a symmetric left/right layout, a naive fixed order would bias one side (the repo already fixed a similar left bias in `snapImpactToNearestMass`). Any GS rewrite needs symmetric sweeps.
- **Rendering/perf**: PBD says nothing about skinning or normals. The measured 15 ms deform cost (skin + cages + `computeVertexNormals`) is untouched by every candidate here; only #4 trims sim time.
