Yes—drivers commonly do **not** start a real demolition derby by immediately reverse-charging the whole field. A basic opening rule in many events is simply that drivers may go forward or backward, but they must make aggressive contact within the time limit, so the first move is usually whatever creates a legal hit fastest and safest[4][7][10][14].

Common opening tactics are usually:

- **Protect the engine and radiator in front** by avoiding head-on exposure unless rules or the car’s build make it worthwhile[8][15].
- **Use the rear as the striking end** in many full-size derbies, since the front is often the most important part to preserve and the rear can be used to deliver hits while steering away from damage[8].
- **Turn and angle into hits** rather than driving straight at opponents, because experienced drivers try to deliver hard contact while limiting damage to their own vulnerable areas[15].
- **Target weaker or already-damaged cars** instead of immediately brawling with everyone at once[15].
- **Get a legal first hit quickly** because many rules require contact within 60–120 seconds or the car can be disqualified[2][3][4][7][10][14].

So the short answer is: **reverse-charging right away is common in some events, but it is not universal and is usually a tactical choice, not a rule**. The main reasons are to protect the engine/front end, keep the car mobile, and meet the event’s mandatory-contact requirement[8][10][14][15].
