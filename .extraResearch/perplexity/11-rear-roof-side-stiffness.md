## (a) FMVSS 216a roof crush and IIHS roof-strength thresholds

- FMVSS 216a requires the roof to resist a maximum applied force equal to **3.0× unloaded vehicle weight** for vehicles with GVWR up to 2,722 kg, and **1.5× unloaded vehicle weight** for vehicles from 2,722 kg to 4,536 kg[5][7].
- FMVSS 216a also requires the lower surface of the test device to move **no more than 127 mm (5 inches)**[5][10].
- IIHS roof-strength ratings use the peak strength-to-weight ratio measured within **127 mm** of platen displacement[1][4][8].
- IIHS rating thresholds are **Good ≥ 4.00**, **Acceptable ≥ 3.25 to < 4.00**, **Marginal ≥ 2.50 to < 3.25**, and **Poor < 2.50**[1][4][6][8].

## (b) FMVSS 301 rear impact

- The upgraded FMVSS 301 rear-impact test strikes the rear of the vehicle at **80 km/h (50 mph)** with a **1,368 kg (3,015 lb) moving deformable barrier** at **70% overlap**[9][12][14].
- The search results did **not** return a primary-source value for a “typical measured rear crush depth” in that test.
- The search results also did **not** return primary-source NHTSA/SAE rear crush stiffness coefficients \(A,B\) for passenger cars, nor a sourced comparison to front stiffness coefficients.

## (c) Side impact / FMVSS 214

- The search results did **not** return primary-source NHTSA side crush stiffness coefficients \(A,B\) for passenger-car door/side structure.
- The search results did **not** return a primary-source “typical residual side crush depth” for the FMVSS 214 MDB test at 62 km/h.
- The search results did **not** return primary-source FMVSS 214 static door-crush step requirements for **10 kN over 152 mm**, **15.6 kN over 305 mm**, or **2× vehicle weight / 53 kN over 457 mm**.

## (d) Typical torsional stiffness of sedans
