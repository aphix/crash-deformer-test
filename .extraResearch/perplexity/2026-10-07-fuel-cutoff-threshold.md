For **Ford-style inertia fuel-pump shutoffs**, the commonly cited trip level is about **10–12 g** on a short crash pulse, with the switch acting very quickly once that threshold is exceeded.[1][8] For **airbag-triggered ECU fuel-pump shutoff**, the shutoff is tied to the crash sensor/airbag deployment threshold rather than a separate fuel-switch threshold; airbag systems are typically calibrated around **3–5 g** acceleration, and deployment is often associated with a crash equivalent to a **rigid barrier at roughly 8–14 mph (about 13–23 km/h) delta-v** for an unbelted frontal crash.[12][13][10]

## Practical interpretation
- **Mechanical inertia switch:** about **10–12 g**[1][8]
- **Airbag/crash-sensor shutoff:** about **3–5 g** sensor-level threshold, but only in the specific crash pulse shape used by the manufacturer’s algorithm[12][13]
- **Equivalent crash severity:** roughly **13–23 km/h delta-v** for the common “moderate-to-severe” frontal deployment reference case[10][12][13]

## Important caveat
There is **no single universal g threshold** or pulse duration for all cars, because manufacturers calibrate these systems differently and the triggering depends heavily on **pulse shape, direction, and duration**, not just peak acceleration.[10][12][13]
