For a modern 1400–1600 kg passenger sedan, published values vary a lot by vehicle and test setup, but the typical ranges below are consistent with NHTSA/IIHS protocol documents and representative IIHS vehicle data. The IIHS moderate-overlap structural ratings use intrusion thresholds in centimeters, while NHTSA/SAE sources more often report crush depth and derived stiffness rather than a single universal intrusion set. [1][2][3][4]

## 1) 56 km/h full-width rigid barrier NCAP test

- **Maximum dynamic crush, front end:** typically about **350–550 mm** for a midsize sedan, with lower values for stiffer structures and higher values for softer front ends. NHTSA’s NCAP rigid-barrier procedure uses this test to estimate stiffness from maximum dynamic crush. [3][12]
- **Permanent static crush:** typically about **250–450 mm** after rebound, often around **70%–90% of dynamic crush** depending on the vehicle’s front structure and energy absorption. [3][12]
- **Crash pulse duration:** typically about **90–140 ms** for the major deceleration event in a full-width rigid-barrier sedan test. NHTSA describes this as a short-duration, high-deceleration “stiff” pulse. [3]
- **Average deceleration at the occupant compartment region:** typically about **18–25 g** over the main pulse for a modern midsize sedan. [3]
- **Peak deceleration:** commonly about **30–45 g**, with some vehicles outside that range depending on stiffness and pulse shape. [3]
- **Rebound velocity:** typically about **1–4 m/s** after the crush phase, varying with stiffness and rebound energy. This is a commonly reported reconstruction/measurement output rather than a fixed NCAP metric. [12]
- **Toe-pan / footwell intrusion:** typically **small in full-width rigid tests**, often around **0–20 mm** in a modern sedan unless the footwell structure is weak. [3]
- **A-pillar / steering column intrusion:** typically **0–15 mm** in a well-designed sedan in this test mode, with much larger values indicating major structural collapse or unusual load paths. [3]

## 2) 64 km/h 40% offset deformable barrier (IIHS moderate overlap / Euro NCAP ODB)

- **Typical residual crush depth on the struck side:** often about **300–600 mm** in the front corner/rail region for a modern sedan, depending on how much the crash is managed by the longitudinal rails versus wheelhouse/hinge pillar load paths. [2]
- **IIHS structural rating intrusion thresholds:** the protocol documents measure the listed locations in centimeters and then apply rating guidelines; the exact thresholds are encoded in IIHS rating tables/figures rather than as a single text list in the protocol. The measured locations are footrest, toepan, brake pedal, rocker panel, steering column, upper/lower hinge pillar, upper dash, and instrument panel. [1][2]

A practical summary of the **IIHS structural rating bands** used for moderate-overlap-type intrusion assessments is:

- **Good:** generally **0–5 cm** at most key structure points, with very small pedal/footwell movement. [1][2]
- **Acceptable:** generally about **5–10 cm**. [1][2]
- **Marginal:** generally about **10–15 cm**. [1][2]
- **Poor:** generally **>15 cm**. [1][2]

Representative published IIHS vehicle data show many modern sedans with moderate-overlap values such as **footrest 1–13 cm, toepan 1–13 cm, brake pedal 2–8 cm, rocker panel lateral average 1–3 cm, steering column 3–5 cm, upper hinge pillar 1–12 cm, and upper dash / lower instrument panel 2–9 cm**, with stronger vehicles toward the low end and weaker ones toward the high end. [4][6][7][10][13][14][15]

## 3) NHTSA frontal crush stiffness coefficients for passenger cars

- NHTSA/SAE crash-reconstruction work commonly uses the frontal force–crush relationship \(F = A x + B x^2\), where **A** is the linear stiffness term and **B** is the quadratic stiffness term. [5][8][9][12]
- For **passenger cars**, a representative published average from NHTSA-related work is roughly:
  - **A:** about **1,336 N/mm** for passenger cars overall, which is **133.6 N/cm**. [8]
  - **B:** reported in the same family of studies as the quadratic term; values vary by vehicle class and front-drive category, and NHTSA reconstruction guidance notes that front-wheel-drive passenger-car stiffness is often treated as a separate category. [9][12]
- Another NHTSA-related summary reports an average **initial linear stiffness** of **1,336 N/mm** for passenger cars since MY2002, with higher averages for LTVs. [8]

## Short takeaway

- **Full-width rigid barrier (56 km/h):** expect **~350–550 mm dynamic crush**, **~250–450 mm permanent crush**, **~90–140 ms pulse**, **~18–25 g average**, **~30–45 g peak**, and usually **small footwell/A-pillar intrusion** in a modern sedan. [3][12]
- **Moderate overlap (64 km/h, 40%):** struck-side residual crush is often **~300–600 mm**, while IIHS structural intrusion bands are roughly **Good 0–5 cm**, **Acceptable 5–10 cm**, **Marginal 10–15 cm**, **Poor >15 cm** for the key measured locations. [1][2][4][6][7][10][13][14][15]
- **Passenger-car stiffness coefficients:** NHTSA-related published passenger-car averages are about **A ≈ 1336 N/mm** and class-dependent **B** values from the crash-stiffness model. [8][9][12]

If a specific sedan class is needed—compact, midsize, or large—the numbers can be tightened to a narrower published range.
