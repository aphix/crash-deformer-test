Here are specific rule-book examples that match the issues you asked about, with the exact timing/door-hit language visible in the published rules.

- **Intentional driver’s-door hits**
- Bond County Fair rules: “**NO HITTING IN DRIVER’S DOOR** … If the hit is deemed by the officials to be careless or intentional, you will be disqualified.” It also says repeated use of the driver’s door as a defense can lead to disqualification at the judge’s discretion.[1]
- Calhoun County Fair rules: “**Hitting of the Driver’s door or head on crashes will lead to disqualification and possible suspension.**”[2]
- Clay County Fair rules: “**NO intentional hitting of the driver’s door. Driver will be disqualified if this happens** …” and “Hitting any open driver’s door will result in disqualification.”[8]
- Wasco County Fair rules: “**NO DELIBERATELY RAMMING A DRIVER’S DOOR. THIS CAN LEAD TO DISQUALIFICATION.**”[10]
- Allegany County Fair rules: “**NO INTENTIONAL hitting on the driver’s door. Doing this will result in disqualification.**”[11][12]

- **Door marked/painted**
- Alaska State Fair rules require that “**Any reasonable number MUST be painted on doors. DRIVER’S DOOR MUST BE RED.**” They also say if the car’s primary color is red, the driver’s door will be painted black.[5]
- Bond County rules prohibit painting on the frame and say if the officials can tell it’s been painted, the car will not be inspected.[1]
- These examples show the common safety practice of marking the driver door, but the exact color/marking requirement varies by book.[1][5]

- **Hit timer / sandbagging clock**
- Clay County Fair: “**NO sandbagging or holding**” and “You will be given **30 seconds for aggressive hits** …”[8]
- El Paso County Fair: “Drivers must make a hit **every minute** … Patty cake hits and sandbagging are grounds for disqualification.”[4]
- Alaska State Fair: “**After 2 minutes without a hit, you will be disqualified.**”[5]
- Herkimer County Fair: “A **90 second time limit** will be enforced. Competitive contact must be made within this time limit …”[3]
- Wasco County Fair: “A **time limit of 60 seconds** will be set by the judges. If a driver does not hit within the sixty seconds they will be timed out.”[10]
- Monroe County Fair: drivers must “hit/make contact with other running cars **every 30 seconds** or may be disqualified.”[9]

- **Head-on hits / whether a forward hit is required**
- Calhoun County Fair: head-on crashes are disqualification-worthy.[2]
- Wasco County Fair: “**NO DELIBERATE HEAD ON COLLISIONS**” and no deliberately ramming a driver’s door.[10]
- Monroe County Fair: “Driver’s door/head-on hits not allowed and may result in disqualification.”[9]
- In the rules surfaced here, the books generally prohibit deliberate head-on shots rather than requiring a forward hit; the common requirement is simply that you make an **aggressive hit on another live car** within the time limit.[2][4][5][8][9][10]

- **Heat time limits / how the heat ends when the last cars stall**
- El Paso County Fair: if the “final two cars in a heat become stuck together for one minute” after a double hit, they are pulled apart; if stuck from a single hit, the moving vehicle is declared the winner.[4]
- Monroe County Fair: “Heat races will have a time limit,” and if more than 3 cars remain when time expires, no cars from that heat transfer to the feature.[9]
- Herkimer County Fair sets a 90-second make-contact rule, but the surfaced snippet does not spell out the last-car-stalling procedure beyond the time limit.[3]
- In practice, when the last cars stall, the rule books shown rely on officials’ judgment of whether a car is still “live,” “moving,” or making competitive contact rather than a universal countdown system.[2][4][5][9][10]

- **Dead car / stalled car counting**
- El Paso County Fair: a stalled car gets “**1 minute to get restarted and 1 minute to make a hit** for a total of 2 minutes” before it is clocked out.[4]
- Clay County Fair: “Any car which does not move or hit a ‘LIVE’ car within **1 minute** will be disqualified.”[8]
- Allegany County Fair: “Any car which does not move or hit a ‘LIVE’ car within **1 minute** will be disqualified.”[11][12]
- Alaska State Fair: “After **2 minutes without a hit**, you will be disqualified.”[5]
- Herkimer County Fair: 90 seconds to make competitive contact, with a 60-second allowance on a second stall.[3]
- Wasco County Fair: 60-second limit to hit; if not, timed out.[10]

## What the published rules most commonly look like
- **Driver’s-door contact is almost always forbidden** and usually brings disqualification if intentional or careless.[1][2][8][10][11][12]
- **The clock is usually 30, 60, 90, or 120 seconds**, depending on the fair and class.[3][4][5][8][10][11][12]
- **Aggressive contact is required**; passive bumping or “sandbagging” is commonly penalized.[4][8][9][10]
- **Head-on hits are usually banned** rather than required.[2][9][10]
