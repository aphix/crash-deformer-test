Real derby rulebooks commonly use a **60-second aggressive-hit rule**, with some events allowing judges to start a count/timer when they judge a car is sandbagging, stuck, or avoiding contact; a few rulebooks use **90 seconds** or **2 minutes** instead. Cars are typically eliminated if they sit immobilized for **60 seconds** or, in some books, **90 seconds** or **2 minutes** without making contact or moving under their own power.[1][2][3][4]

- **1) No-aggressive-hit / mandatory-hit interval**
- **60 seconds** is the most common rule in the rulebooks found: drivers must make an aggressive hit every 60 seconds, or they are timed out/disqualified.[1][3][5][6][7][8]
- Some rules explicitly say the **judge/official starts the count** when they notice sandbagging, being stuck, or hit avoidance.[1][2][4]
- One rulebook sets a **2-minute** limit, stating that cars that do not make contact within two minutes are flagged and timed out, with flagmen using stopwatches.[4]
- Another rulebook uses **90 seconds**: if a driver times out for more than 90 seconds, they must shut the vehicle off and break the stick.[9]

- **2) Immobilized car / no-movement time limit**
- A common immobilization limit is **60 seconds**: one fair rulebook says any vehicle that does not move for one minute will be disqualified.[7][8]
- Another rulebook uses **90 seconds** before a timing-out consequence applies.[9]
- Another uses **2 minutes** if a car does not make contact or remains effectively inactive.[4]

- **3) Other elimination causes**
- **Driver’s door hits**: intentional driver-door hits are commonly grounds for disqualification, sometimes after one warning and a second violation.[1][5][9]
- **Fire / safety issues**: cars deemed unsafe by officials can be stopped and disqualified.[4][7]
- **Broken stick / red-flag violations**: one rulebook says intentional hits after the red flag are disqualifying; another says a timed-out car must shut off and break its stick.[5][9]
- **Sandbagging / hit avoidance**: explicitly disqualifying in several rules, and in one rulebook it triggers the hit clock.[1][2][6]
- **Out of bounds / unsportsmanlike conduct**: leaving the marked area or unsportsmanlike behavior can also remove a car from competition.[1][4]

- **4) How a heat ends**
- In the rules found, the heat usually ends when **the last legal car to make a hit** is the winner, or when **the last car still moving / still in competition** remains after all others are timed out or disqualified.[5][9][10]
- One rulebook states: **“The last car to make the last hit is the winner.”**[5]
- Another says **“The last legal car to make a hit on a competing car is declared the winner.”**[4]
