## 1) Bumper systems: FMVSS 581 / IIHS low-speed tests
- IIHS low-speed bumper tests are run at impact speeds in the low-speed range, and the protocol treats small cover tears/holes as acceptable if they are no more than 1 cm and not in critical fastener areas[11][12][13].
- The IIHS protocol language shows that bumper-cover damage is expected in these tests, but it only becomes disqualifying when tears/holes exceed 1 cm or when cracking/gouging exceeds 2 cm[11][12][13].
- I do not have a source in the retrieved set that gives a precise “clip/fastener failure speed” or a single barrier speed at which bumper covers “typically tear off.”

## 3) FMVSS 206 door latch and hinge strength; door opening in crashes
- FMVSS 206 requires hinged side-door latches to withstand 11,000 N in the longitudinal direction and 8,900 N in the transverse direction in the fully latched position[1][3][5][15].
- FMVSS 206 also requires side-door hinge systems to withstand 11,000 N longitudinally and 8,900 N transversely[6][9][14].
- NHTSA’s 1995 Federal Register notice states FMVSS 206 is intended to minimize occupant ejection through side-door openings and says the standard requires each latch and striker system and each hinge system not to disengage when a longitudinal force of 2,500 lb or a transverse force of 2,000 lb is applied[15].
- I do not have a retrieved NHTSA dataset quantifying the real-world rate of door opening in rollovers or side impacts.
