Driver 2’s Havana Survival mode starts you in a 1950 Chevrolet Styleline De Luxe and immediately throws you into a nonstop police chase on the Havana map[2]. The mode is about lasting as long as possible without getting totaled; in Driver 2 you cannot exit the vehicle, and the run ends when the car is wrecked beyond use[2].

## How the mode plays

- The cops in Survival are much nastier than normal traffic police: they are described as twice as fast, invincible, and heavier in collisions[2].
- You are chased by several police cars at once from the start, and the mode is essentially “survive the onslaught of cops” rather than escape a single pursuit[2][6].
- In Driv3r, the same style of mode returns with the same basic rule set, but the game’s Survival explicitly centers on lasting against “manic cops” that ram and try to wreck you[1][5].

## Chase count and escalation

- The most specific community description for Driv3r says Survival starts with an immediate wave of 4 police cars[12], and Driver 2 Survival is consistently described as multiple police cars chasing you in parallel[2][3].
- Available references here do not confirm a precise Driver 2 Havana police count, a scripted spawn schedule, or a difficulty ramp over time.
- What is clear is that the pressure stays continuous: as you survive, more danger comes from the ongoing pursuit and collisions rather than from a different objective or a timed wave structure[2][3].

## Damage and bust rules

- The player car has a damage state that worsens as cops ram you and force collisions[2][3].
- The mode ends when the vehicle is totaled, not when you are “busted” in the usual police-stop sense[2].
- In Driv3r, the same rule is stated plainly: if the vehicle gets totaled, the game is over[1].

## Havana starting area and layout

- The Havana Survival map starts in Plaza de la Revolución, with the José Martí Memorial tower on the hill, matching the well-known location layout seen by players[8].
- The plaza is remembered as a broad open area with a grassy, ramped hill around the monument, which makes it a strong space for looping, climbing, and forcing AI cars into awkward lines[8].
- Community discussion notes that the maps in Driver 2 are fairly accurate to the real location, just scaled down[8].

## Traffic, roadblocks, and cop behavior

- Traffic in Survival becomes part of the hazard because cops are trying to ram you through ordinary road clutter while you stay in motion[2][3].
- The mode is built around aggressive pursuit and collisions, so roadblocks and traffic are effectively part of the survivability puzzle even when they are not the primary scripted objective[2][3].
- Player reports also describe the cops as unusually chaotic in this mode, with flipping and pileups being a notable quirk of the AI behavior[8].

## Community strategies and quirks

- A commonly mentioned exploit is to drive toward the docks/industrial edge of the map and use the geometry to break pursuit or trap cops[8].
- Another recurring quirk is that police cars can flip or wedge themselves on the plaza’s slopes and curbs, which players use to gain breathing room[8].
- The plaza hill and monument area are especially useful because the AI struggles with the elevation changes and tight turns[8].

## Driv3r comparison

- Driv3r keeps Survival as a return of the classic Driver challenge: survive as long as possible while police try to destroy your car[1][5].
- In Driv3r, the mode is described as having 4 police cars at once, and one guide notes that the cops are especially aggressive and crash-prone[12][9].
- The main difference is presentation and handling, but the core idea remains the same: stay alive in a hostile pursuit until your car is totaled[1][12].
